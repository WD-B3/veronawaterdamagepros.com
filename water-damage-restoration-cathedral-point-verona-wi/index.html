Cathedral Point is a planned residential neighborhood on the south side of Verona. Local real estate guides place it near the US 18 and 151 corridor and list mostly single-family homes, including areas called Woods at Cathedral Point and Cathedral Point 1st Addition. City of Verona park records put Cathedral Point Park, an 8 acre neighborhood park, at Westminster Way and Chads Crossing.

If water is spreading through a Cathedral Point home right now, call the number on this page and tell dispatch which rooms it has reached. Dispatch cannot promise an arrival time, and will say plainly what to expect for your address.

Finding Cathedral Point on the map

No official boundary map is cited on this page, so use landmarks. Westminster Way, Chads Crossing and Cathedral Point Park are the reference points in the city records. Real estate guides also place Glacier Edge Elementary near the neighborhood.

When you call, give your street address and the nearest cross street. That lets dispatch judge the route and tell you honestly whether a crew can reach you soon.

What is known about water here

Flood-zone status has not been verified for Cathedral Point as a whole. A FEMA map zone applies to one parcel, so check your own address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center. No source reviewed for this page names Cathedral Point as a flood-prone area.

The regional record is a separate matter. According to the National Weather Service, rain gauge reports around Verona during the storm of August 20 and 21, 2018 ranged from 6.76 to 11.02 inches. That record does not say whether any Cathedral Point home took water.

Household sources in single-family homes

In a neighborhood of single-family houses, water damage can start inside the home. A washing machine hose lets go, a water heater tank weeps at the base, a dishwasher line drips under the cabinet, or a toilet supply line fails behind the wall.

These losses are often slow at first and then sudden. A damp patch on a ceiling below an upstairs bathroom is a sign to act, because the water has already traveled through the floor.

First steps before help arrives

Find the shutoff

Close the valve at the fixture or the main shutoff near the meter. If you cannot reach it safely, say so when you call.

Stay clear of power

Do not stand in water near outlets, cords or the panel. If lights flicker, step back and call the utility.

Lift what you can

Raise electronics, books and boxes off wet floors, and pull rugs up if that is safe.

Photograph first

Take wide room photos and a short video before anything is moved. Note the date.

How drying works after the water is gone

Removing standing water is the first stage. The second is drying the materials that soaked it up, such as carpet pad, drywall bottoms, subfloor and framing. A crew uses moisture meters to read the wood and drywall, then sets air movers and dehumidifiers.

Equipment usually runs for several days. Readings are taken again before it is removed, and the number of days depends on what was wet and for how long.

Paperwork and your insurance policy

Coverage depends on the cause of the loss and the terms of your policy, so read the policy and call your insurer or agent. Photos, moisture readings and an itemized scope can help describe the damage. None of them guarantees a claim will be paid.

Safety until a crew arrives

  • Keep people and pets out of any water that may touch live electrical parts.
  • Do not use a household vacuum on standing water.
  • Treat water from a sewer line or drain as contaminated and stay out of it.
  • Leave suspected asbestos or lead materials undisturbed.

Cathedral Point water damage questions

Is Cathedral Point in a flood zone?

That has not been verified for the whole neighborhood. FEMA maps apply to parcels, so use your own address.

What usually causes water damage in a single-family home here?

Household sources such as supply lines, appliances and water heaters are common in any such neighborhood. Storm leaks and sump failures are the other main routes. A site visit is the only way to know about your house.

How do I describe my location to dispatch?

Give the street address and the nearest cross street. Westminster Way and Chads Crossing near Cathedral Point Park are useful reference points.

Does homeowners insurance cover water damage?

It depends on the cause and the policy. Sudden pipe or appliance failures are treated differently from long-term seepage or groundwater, so ask your insurer before you decide.

Can I dry the carpet myself?

A small clean-water spill can be handled with towels and fans. Larger or longer-lasting water, or anything from a drain, needs professional drying and cleanup.
