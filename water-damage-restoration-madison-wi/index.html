What water damage looks like on the isthmus and beyond

Madison sits on an isthmus between Lake Mendota and Lake Monona, with older neighborhoods close to the water and newer ones spread west and east. Housing ranges from early 20th century homes to modern apartments and commercial space. Age matters because older plumbing, older basements and older roofs fail in different ways.

Apartments and condos add a second layer, since water from one unit can reach several others. If that is your situation, tell dispatch which floor the water started on and which units it reached.

Madison water and weather facts

Madison’s 1991 to 2020 climate normals list 37.13 inches of precipitation, 51.8 inches of snow and a 19.4°F January mean. Heavy rain, snowmelt and hard freezes all put pressure on homes.

On August 20 and 21, 2018, heavy rain across Dane County flooded areas around the Yahara chain of lakes. On June 17, 2014, tornadoes touched down in the Madison area, including an EF-2 in southwest Madison and an EF-1 in the Marquette neighborhood, according to the National Weather Service. Check the FEMA flood map for your address, and ask the City of Madison about its sump pump discharge rules before you point a hose anywhere.

Before We Arrive

Find the valve

Locate the shutoff under the fixture or at the main. In an apartment, ask the building manager where the unit shutoff is.

Mind the outlets

Do not wade through water near outlets or cords. If the lights flicker, step back and call the utility.

Lift valuables

Raise electronics, books and paper onto counters and beds, and pull rugs off wet floors.

Take pictures

Shoot wide room photos and a short walkthrough video before anything is moved.

Cost and insurance in Madison

Cost depends on how long the water sat, how many rooms and floors it reached, what materials are wet, and how many days of drying are needed.
