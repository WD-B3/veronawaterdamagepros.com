Hawthorn Hills is a residential neighborhood in southeast Verona. City of Verona park records place Tollefson Park at Gatsby Glen Drive and Whalen Road and Tower Park at Fairview Terrace and Marlow Bay Drive, both within Hawthorn Hills. The records say Tollefson Park also serves Glacier Edge Elementary and the adjacent neighborhood.

Some real estate guides spell the name Hawthorne Hills and describe a subdivision built mostly between 2005 and 2010. If water has reached your home, call and tell dispatch where it entered.

Around Tollefson and Tower parks

Use the parks and streets as reference points when you call. Gatsby Glen Drive, Whalen Road, Fairview Terrace and Marlow Bay Drive are named in the city records.

No official boundary is cited here. If your street is near the edge of the neighborhood, give the nearest cross street so dispatch can locate you correctly.

Rain on the roof and on the ground

A newer subdivision sheds rain through gutters, downspouts and graded lots. When a gutter clogs or a downspout empties against a foundation, water finds its way to the lowest point. That can be a window well, a garage floor or a finished lower level.

After a heavy storm, walk the outside of the house. Look for ponding along the foundation and for downspouts that end too close to the wall. Photograph anything you find before it dries.

Storm records near Verona

According to the National Weather Service, slow-moving thunderstorms across Madison and nearby communities from late on June 15 into the early hours of June 16, 2018. Reports near Verona were 2.25 to 2.56 inches, and the record does not describe flooding in Verona. Water did enter some homes on Madison’s southwest side.

Heavier rain fell on August 20 and 21, 2018, with gauge reports around Verona from 6.76 to 11.02 inches. Neither record says what happened in Hawthorn Hills. No neighborhood-wide FEMA flood-zone finding was verified for Hawthorn Hills. Enter your address at the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for a parcel-level answer.

What the first call covers

Dispatch asks where the water came from, how deep it is and whether power or a furnace is involved. You get plain directions on what to do while a crew is arranged. Dispatch does not promise an arrival time.

Check the sump

If the pit is rising or the pump is silent, note the level and say so when you call.

Watch the outlets

Stay out of lower levels where water may touch outlets or the panel.

Follow the water uphill

Find where it entered, such as a window well, door or wall, and photograph that spot.

Move things up

Lift boxes, furniture legs and electronics off wet floors if it is safe.

Warranties and repairs

Homes built in the 2000s may still have appliance or home warranties in force. Read any warranty before repairs begin, since it may name who must approve the work. A warranty is a contract and not a promise from this site.

For emergency steps such as stopping the water and drying, keep receipts and take photos before you call anyone.

Insurance notes

Insurers separate sudden storm or plumbing losses from groundwater or long-term seepage. Read your policy and call your agent early. Ask whether sump pump overflow is covered, since many policies treat it as a separate endorsement.

Staying safe after a storm

  • Do not walk into water that may touch outlets, cords or the panel.
  • Stay out from under a ceiling that is bulging or dripping.
  • Never run a generator indoors or in an attached garage.
  • Call the utility for downed lines or gas smell.

Hawthorn Hills water damage questions

Is it Hawthorn Hills or Hawthorne Hills?

The City of Verona park records use Hawthorn Hills. Some real estate sites spell it Hawthorne. Either way, give dispatch your street address.

Was Hawthorn Hills flooded in 2018?

The sources reviewed do not say. They record heavy rain near Verona and flooding elsewhere in Dane County, but nothing specific to this neighborhood.

Why did my finished lower level get wet?

Common routes are a sump that failed, a window well that filled, or water entering at the wall and floor joint. A site visit finds the actual entry point.

Should I call my insurer first?

Stopping the water and limiting damage comes first. Then contact your insurer promptly, and keep photos and receipts.

Can a ceiling stain be ignored?

A dry, old stain may be harmless, but a growing or dripping one means water is still present. Have it checked soon.
