How to Prevent Water Damage When You Leave Home in Verona, WI

How to prevent water damage when you leave home for a few days or a whole winter in Verona, WI. A practical checklist of shutoffs, heat and checks.

Written by the Verona Water Damage Pros team. Published November 9, 2026.
DISPATCH LINE
(608) 871-6605
Call Now

Most large water losses grow because nobody was home to stop them. A line that splits on day one of a vacation can run until you return. A short checklist before you leave reduces that risk a lot. It is written for Verona, WI homeowners, with extra attention to Wisconsin winters.

Before you go: the checklist

Step 01

Know and test your main shutoff

Find the main water valve and turn it. A valve that is stuck is no help in an emergency.

Step 02

Shut off the water for longer trips

If you will be away several days, closing the main valve limits the harm from a failed line. Open a faucet to drain the lines if the house will be cold.

Step 03

Check appliance hoses

Washer hoses and dishwasher lines fail without warning. Look for bulges, cracks and rust at the fittings.

Step 04

Test the sump pump

Pour a bucket of water into the pit and watch the pump start and stop.

Step 05

Clear gutters and downspouts

Make sure they carry water away from the foundation.

Step 06

Set the thermostat

In cold weather, keep the heat on at a safe level. Many policies expect it.

Winter is the high risk season

Madison’s 1991 to 2020 climate normals show a January mean of 19.4°F and 51.8 inches of snow a year. Long cold stretches mean pipes in exterior walls, garages and crawl spaces are at risk. Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls, and do not turn the heat off to save money.

If you leave for a winter, ask a neighbor or friend to check the house on a schedule. A visit every few days catches a failure before it grows.

Verona preparation notes

Dane County has had storm events that tested local drainage, including the August 20 and 21, 2018 floods that brought 11 to 15 inches of rain to parts of western Dane County. If you will be away in a stormy season, check your sump pump and gutters before you go.

Ask the City of Verona building department about sump discharge rules before changing where your pump drains.

Who should have a key and the number

Leave a house key and the dispatch number with a trusted person. Tell them where the main shutoff is. If they find water, they should shut it off, photograph it and call the 24/7 line before they call you.

Tell your insurer if you will be away for an extended period. Some policies have vacancy conditions that affect coverage.

If you come home to water

  • Do not enter if water is near outlets or the panel is wet.
  • Close the main valve and photograph everything.
  • Call the 24/7 line and tell dispatch how long you were gone.
  • Call your insurer as soon as it is safe.

A fast water extraction response limits damage while you were away. If the cause was a split line, see the frozen pipe page. Crews cover Mount Horeb and Oregon as well.

A short routine for each season

In spring, check the sump pump and clear gutters. In summer, look at washer hoses and the water heater. In fall, disconnect garden hoses and insulate exposed pipes. In winter, keep the heat on and open cabinet doors on exterior walls during extreme cold.

A quarterly check takes ten minutes and finds slow leaks while they are small. Look under sinks for dampness, check the ceiling under bathrooms for stains and watch the water meter for movement when everything is off.

If the meter moves with all fixtures off, water is going somewhere. That is a sign to call a plumber, and a reason to find out where before you leave town.

Write the plan down and tape it inside a cabinet door: where the main valve is, who has a key and which number to call. A sitter or a relative can follow it without phoning you.

According to the EPA’s guidance on indoor humidity, keeping relative humidity below about 60 percent helps prevent mold growth, which matters if the house sits closed up for weeks.

Away from home questions

Should I shut off the water when I go on vacation?

For trips of several days, many homeowners do. It limits the harm from a failed line. Leave the water heater setting at a safe level or off, as the manufacturer advises.

What temperature should I keep the house in a Wisconsin winter?

Keep the heat on at a level your insurer and plumber consider safe. Do not turn it off, since pipes in exterior walls can freeze.

Do water sensors really help?

They help when someone receives and acts on the alert. A sensor with no one to read it only makes noise.

What if I find water after being away for weeks?

Close the main valve, do not walk through standing water near power and call the 24/7 line.
