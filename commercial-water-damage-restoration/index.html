Commercial water damage restoration covers water losses in shops, offices, restaurants, warehouses and multi-unit buildings, where every hour of closure costs money. If a pipe has failed or a roof has leaked at a Verona business, call the 24/7 line now. The work follows the same ANSI/IICRC S500 principles as a home, but the stakes are different because inventory, tenants and operations are involved.

A homeowner can move to a hotel for a week. A business has staff, customers and deadlines, so the plan starts with what must stay open.

When a business water loss is an emergency

Treat a commercial loss as an emergency when water reaches inventory, electrical equipment, server rooms, food areas or customer space. Shut off the water at the main or the local valve, keep staff and customers out of wet areas and protect equipment that can be moved. If power is involved, have a qualified electrician or the utility make it safe before anyone works in the water.

For managers and owners right now

  • Close the water supply to the affected area or the whole building.
  • Move inventory, files and electronics off the floor and away from the water.
  • Photograph the damage and note times for your insurance records.
  • Tell tenants, staff and your landlord or property manager who needs to know.
  • Call the 24/7 line and describe the building type and how many areas are wet.

After-hours calls are common for commercial losses because pipes fail overnight and on weekends. Dispatch answers every hour, and a crew can be sent the same day when one is available.

How commercial losses differ from home losses

Large floor plates

Open floors and long wall runs spread water over more square footage, so moisture mapping covers more ground.

Business continuity

Phasing the work lets part of the space reopen while another is dried.

Multi-party coordination

Landlords, tenants, property managers and insurers all need clear documentation.

Specialized contents

Inventory, paper records and equipment need handling that a living room does not.

Multi-unit buildings add a second problem. Water from one unit often reaches the one below, so a leak that looks small can involve several residents and several insurers. Records of which rooms were wet and when help sort that out.

Local note for Verona businesses

Commercial buildings range from older storefronts to newer space, and water failures differ between them. Older plumbing and flat roofs fail one way, and newer sprinkler and rooftop equipment fail another. Dane County also saw heavy storm flooding in August 2018, when 11 to 15 inches of rain fell on parts of western Dane County, a reminder that parking lots and roofs shed water fast.

Ask your landlord who is responsible for roof and drain maintenance, and check your commercial policy for business interruption coverage before you need it. If the loss came from a storm opening, the storm water damage page adds detail.

What affects commercial restoration cost

  • Square footage and the number of rooms or tenants affected.
  • Water category, especially if sewage or flood water is involved.
  • Building materials, such as slab, framed walls, suspended ceilings and flooring.
  • Contents handling, from inventory to records.
  • Drying days and the equipment that requires.
  • Phasing needs, if parts of the building must stay open.

Commercial water damage questions

Do you handle commercial water emergencies at night or on weekends in Verona?

Yes. Dispatch answers 24/7, and a crew can be sent the same day when one is available. Commercial calls are often after hours, and the line treats them as normal.

Can part of my business stay open?

Often yes. If the loss is limited to certain zones, crews can contain and dry those areas while the rest operates. A site visit confirms what is realistic.

Do you help with documentation for my insurer?

We keep moisture readings, photos and scope notes as the job moves. Your insurer decides coverage, and we do not promise approval.

Do you work on apartment buildings and other multi-unit properties?

Yes. Multi-unit losses involve several residents, so documentation of every wet unit is a priority. Call and describe how many units the water reached.
