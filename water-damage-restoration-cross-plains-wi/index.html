The creek, the rain and the basement

Black Earth Creek runs through the Cross Plains area, and it rises fast in a big storm. On August 20 and 21, 2018, 11 to 15 inches of rain fell in parts of western Dane County, and flooding followed along the creek. That event is a reminder that low spots, window wells and sump pumps matter here.

If your basement takes in water after heavy rain, the usual pattern is seepage at the wall and floor joint, a sump pump that cannot keep up, or a window well that fills and spills.

Cross Plains conditions

Cross Plains is in the same Dane County rain belt that took the 2018 storms. The Madison record for 1991 to 2020 shows 37.13 inches of precipitation a year and 51.8 inches of snow, so spring thaws and summer storms both test basements.

Check the FEMA flood map for your parcel, and ask the Village of Cross Plains or Dane County about local drainage rules before changing grading or sump discharge.

Sump pumps, window wells and the spring thaw

A sump pump is only useful if it runs when the power is out and the pit is filling. Pumps fail from age, stuck floats and storm outages, and the basement floods while the owner is at work. Test the pump before the wet season by pouring a bucket of water into the pit.

Window wells fill when a downspout dumps beside them. Covers and clean gravel help, and a drain at the bottom keeps them from becoming bathtubs against the glass.
