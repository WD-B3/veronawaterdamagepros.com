How to Save Photos and Documents After Water Damage in Verona, WI

Wet photos and papers can often be saved if you act quickly. Here is what conservators advise for Verona, WI homeowners, step by step.

Written by the Verona Water Damage Pros team. Published November 9, 2026.

Family photos and key documents can feel more important than anything else in a flooded room. Many can be saved if you act within a day or two. This guide follows the general advice of conservators and archivists. It is not a substitute for a professional if something is rare or valuable.

First: make sure it is safe to handle

If the water was sewage or flood water, wear gloves and a mask, and treat the items as contaminated. Do not scrape or rub wet photos and paper, because the surface is fragile. Handle by the edges, and support the weight of heavy items.

Photographs

Step 01

Rinse gently

If photos are muddy, rinse them in clean water. Do not rub the surface.

Step 02

Separate

Peel apart photos that stuck together while they are still wet and underwater, if they come apart easily. If they resist, leave them for a conservator.

Step 03

Air dry

Lay photos face up on clean paper towels or a screen, away from direct sun. Change the towels as they absorb water.

Step 04

Freeze if you cannot dry in time

Wrap in wax paper, place in a freezer bag and freeze. That stops mold and buys time.

Freezing is the most useful tool. It halts mold and lets you deal with items in batches, not all at once. A chest freezer in a garage works as well as a kitchen one.

Which documents to replace first

  • Photo ID, passports and vital records, which can be reissued.
  • Insurance policies, which your agent can copy.
  • Tax papers, which can be requested from the tax authorities.
  • Deeds and mortgage papers, which are held on record.
  • Warranty and appliance manuals, which are often online.

Record what was lost and how. Many replacement agencies ask for a statement. Your insurer may also need a list.

Verona records notes

Property records such as deeds are held by the county Register of Deeds, so a damaged copy can be replaced. Check with Dane County for the process.

If your home flooded in a major local event, such as the August 20 and 21, 2018 rain that fell on parts of western Dane County, check with local library or historical society staff. They sometimes share advice for rescuing damaged family papers.

When to call a conservator

A conservator is trained to treat historic and valuable items. Call one for rare photographs, old documents, art or heirloom books. Freeze the item first, and ask about next steps. Cost varies, so ask for an estimate.

For ordinary family prints, drying at home is often fine. Make digital copies as soon as the photos are dry.

Protecting items next time

  • Store important papers in a waterproof, fire rated container above floor level.
  • Scan photos and key documents and keep a second copy in cloud storage.
  • Avoid basement floors for storage of paper and photographs.
  • Keep a short list of what is where.

For help getting the water out so the room can dry, see water extraction or the basement cleanup page. Crews cover Mount Horeb and Oregon too.

Digital backups, scans and an inventory

After the emergency is over, scan what you saved. A phone camera in good light works for most documents. Name the files by date and type, and store a copy somewhere that is not in your house, such as a cloud account or a relative’s computer.

For photos, scan at a higher resolution than you think you need. Prints that survived a flood often keep fading, and a scan preserves what is there today.

Label the folder or box clearly, and tell a family member where it is kept. A rescue plan only works if someone else can find the box, and a short label saves time when you are stressed.

Make a short written inventory of what is where, including the location of the fire rated box, the safe deposit box key and the names of your insurance policies. Share it with one trusted person. In an emergency, that list saves hours.

According to common conservation guidelines, freezing wet paper and photographs stops mold and buys time until they can be dried in batches.

Photos and documents questions

Can I put wet photos in the freezer?

Yes. Wrap them in wax paper, place them in a freezer bag and freeze. Thaw and dry them in batches later.

Where do I get a replacement for a flooded deed in Dane County?

Contact the county Register of Deeds. Records are held on file, so damaged copies can be replaced.

Can a hair dryer dry a photo?

Heat can warp and stick photos. Air drying face up on clean paper is safer.

Will mold grow on my papers?

It can within a few days of staying damp. Dry or freeze quickly.
