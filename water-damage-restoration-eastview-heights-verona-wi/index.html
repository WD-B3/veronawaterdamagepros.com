Eastview Heights is a residential subdivision in southeast Verona. City of Verona park records place Prairie View Park at Forest View Drive and Harvest Lane, and Vande Grift Park on Parkland Drive, both in Eastview Heights. Some real estate sites spell it East View Heights.

A real estate guide describes most homes as built between 1981 and 2000, with quick access to US 18 and 151. If water has reached your home, call and tell dispatch what it has touched.

Southeast Verona and its parks

Forest View Drive, Harvest Lane and Parkland Drive are the reference points in the city records. No official boundary is cited here, so give the nearest cross street when you call.

Winter in Eastview Heights

Verona winters are cold. According to the National Weather Service, 1991 to 2020 normals for Madison list a January mean of 19.4°F and 51.8 inches of snow a year. Pipes in outside walls, garages and unheated spaces carry the most risk when temperatures drop.

A frozen pipe often shows up as a trickle or no flow at a faucet. The damage comes when the ice thaws and the split pipe starts to leak.

Water facts for this area

Annual precipitation in the Madison normals is 37.13 inches, a useful reference for the region. Nothing reviewed for this page reports flooding in Eastview Heights. Eastview Heights has no verified neighborhood-wide FEMA flood-zone finding. Use the FEMA Flood Map Service Center to check a specific address.

The Dane County hazard plan for Verona found no repetitive loss properties in the city as of 2017. That is a city-wide finding and does not speak for any single street.

If a pipe freezes or bursts

Step 01

Shut off the main

Close the main water valve to stop the flow. Know where it is before winter.

Step 02

Open faucets

After the shutoff, open the lowest faucet to let the line drain.

Step 03

Keep clear of power

Stay out of wet areas with live outlets, and call the utility if unsure.

Step 04

Call and describe

Tell dispatch how much water came out and which rooms are wet.

Dampness after the thaw

Snowmelt can send water toward foundations in spring. If a basement wall shows dampness after a thaw, note where it appears and how often. A pattern points to a drainage cause such as a clogged gutter or a window well, and the fix may be outside the house.

Insurance and your records

Insurers treat a sudden pipe failure differently from seepage or groundwater. Read your policy and ask your agent early. Photos, receipts and drying logs can help describe the work, and they do not guarantee that a claim is paid.

Cold weather safety steps

  • Never use a flame or torch to thaw a pipe.
  • Stay out of water that touches outlets, cords or the panel.
  • Keep children and pets away from wet floors and loose ceilings.
  • If heat is out, protect remaining pipes by opening cabinet doors.

Eastview Heights water damage questions

Are 1980s and 1990s homes more at risk in winter?

Age alone is not proof. Pipes in outside walls and unheated spaces are at risk in any home when it gets cold enough.

Is Eastview Heights flood-prone?

No reviewed source says so. FEMA maps are by parcel, so check your own address.

Is it East View or Eastview Heights?

City park records use Eastview Heights. Some real estate sites write East View Heights. Use your street address with dispatch either way.

What if the pipe is frozen but not leaking yet?

Open the faucet, keep the area warm and avoid open flames. If the pipe splits, close the main and call.

How fast should drying start?

As soon as the water is removed. Delay lets moisture spread into walls and framing.
