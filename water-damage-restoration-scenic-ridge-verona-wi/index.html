Scenic Ridge is a residential neighborhood in Verona. City of Verona park records place Scenic Ridge Park, a 2.2 acre neighborhood park, at Scenic Ridge Drive and Pinehurst Drive. A real estate guide calls it a newer neighborhood with single-family homes in prairie, craftsman, transitional and ranch styles, many with three-car garages.

If water is spreading in a Scenic Ridge home, call and tell dispatch which rooms it has reached. Dispatch cannot promise an arrival time and will say plainly what to expect.

Finding Scenic Ridge

Scenic Ridge Drive and Pinehurst Drive are the street references in the city records. The sources reviewed do not give a compass direction or a boundary, so none is claimed here. Give dispatch your address and nearest cross street.

Roofs, ceilings and attic leaks

Newer homes with larger rooflines have more places for wind-driven rain, ice dams or a failed flashing to let water in. The first sign is often a stain or bubble on a ceiling, sometimes far from the actual opening.

If a ceiling is sagging, stay out from under it. A trapped pool of water can release suddenly. Move furniture away and place a container below any drip.

What the records say and do not say

According to the National Weather Service, the June 17, 2014 tornado near Country View Elementary in Verona was an EF-3. The record does not place damage in Scenic Ridge, so it should be read as a regional event only.

Madison’s 1991 to 2020 normals show 51.8 inches of snow a year, which is why ice dams and roof leaks are worth watching. The FEMA status for Scenic Ridge as a whole was not verified. Map zones follow individual parcels, so search your own address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center.

Garage, utility room and laundry leaks

Homes with large attached garages often put the water heater, softener and laundry in a utility area off the garage. A failed tank or loose fitting there can spread water to the garage slab and the adjoining rooms.

Check these areas after any big temperature swing. Look for rust on the tank base and for damp drywall near the floor.

Close the valve

Shut the water supply to the leaking fixture or the main if it is safe to reach.

Protect the panel

If the leak is above or near the electric panel, stay clear and call the utility.

Contain the drip

Place buckets under ceiling drips and move furniture out from beneath stains.

Take photos

Photograph the stain, any wet floor and the suspected source with the date noted.

Cleaning up and drying out

A crew removes standing water, then opens or dries wet materials depending on what readings show. Wet insulation is usually removed. Drywall that stayed wet is often cut out, and framing is dried in place.

The aim is to dry the structure before moisture settles in. Mold work is a separate service, and this site does not promise to prevent mold.

Insurance basics

Your policy and the cause of the damage decide coverage. Ask your insurer about roof or storm losses and about water heater failures. Photos and an itemized scope help describe the work and do not guarantee payment.

Safety steps for roof and ceiling leaks

  • Stay out from under a bulging ceiling.
  • Never touch a light fixture that has water dripping from it.
  • Do not climb onto a wet or icy roof to look for the leak.
  • Leave suspected asbestos or lead materials alone.

Scenic Ridge water damage questions

Where exactly is Scenic Ridge?

City records place Scenic Ridge Park at Scenic Ridge Drive and Pinehurst Drive. The sources reviewed give no boundary, so use your street address.

Do newer homes have fewer water problems?

Not necessarily. Newer homes have different weak points, such as roof flashing, fittings and appliance lines.

A ceiling stain appeared after rain. What now?

Place a container, stay out from under it and call. The opening in the roof may be far from the stain.

Can I dry a hardwood floor with a fan?

A small clean spill may dry that way. Water that sits under boards needs measured drying, since boards can cup.

Is Scenic Ridge in a flood zone?

The sources reviewed give no neighborhood-wide answer. Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your parcel.
