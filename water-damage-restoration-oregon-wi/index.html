Oregon is a village of 11,179 people south of Madison, where US 14 and WIS 138 meet. If water is spreading in your Oregon home or business, call the 24/7 line. A crew can be sent the same day when one is available, and dispatch does not promise an arrival time.

Older downtown, newer neighborhoods

Oregon has an older core, including the South Main Street Historic District, and newer subdivisions around it. Older buildings often have older plumbing, older roofs and older basements, which fail in different ways than newer ones. Newer homes bring different risks, such as plastic supply lines and finished basements.

Tell dispatch roughly how old the home or building is. It helps the crew plan for what is behind the walls.

Oregon conditions

Oregon shares Dane County weather with Madison, where the 1991 to 2020 normals list 37.13 inches of precipitation and a January mean of 19.4°F. Freezes and thaws are as much a risk here as summer storms.

Check the FEMA flood map for your parcel, and ask the Village of Oregon about local drainage and permit rules.

Before We Arrive

Stop the flow

Close the fixture valve or the main. In a shop or rental, tell the manager or owner at once.

Respect the wiring

Older buildings may have outdated wiring, so keep clear of wet outlets and fixtures.

Clear a path

Move furniture, stock and boxes off the floor to give the crew room to work.

Document the scene

Photograph the damage and any stains on ceilings or walls, with the date noted.

Winter plumbing and the spring thaw

Oregon winters bring long freezes. Pipes in exterior walls, garages and crawl spaces split when the water inside turns to ice, and the real flooding starts when it thaws. Know where your main shutoff is before the cold arrives, and test that it turns.

Roof ice dams cause a different problem. Snow melts, refreezes at the eave and pushes water under shingles and into ceilings and walls.

When the phone is answered

Dispatch asks where the water started, what it has touched and whether power is involved. You get plain direction on what to shut off and what to move. The crew arrives with pumps, extractors and meters, checks walls, floors and ceilings for hidden moisture, and sets up drying equipment with daily readings until the structure is dry.

Older buildings sometimes need extra care around plaster, old wiring and finishes. Tell dispatch what you know about the building.

If you manage a downtown shop or a rental, add how many units or tenants are affected. Water often crosses a shared wall or ceiling, and the crew needs to check the neighbors too.

Paying for the work

Price depends on how much water there is, how long it sat, which materials are wet and how many days of drying are needed. Scope and price are laid out for you before work starts. Insurance treats sudden losses differently from slow leaks, so read your policy and keep photos.

The Wisconsin Department of Safety and Professional Services publishes licensing guidelines for the building trades. It is the place to check which trades need a license for a given repair.
