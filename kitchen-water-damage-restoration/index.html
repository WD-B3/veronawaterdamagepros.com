Kitchen water damage restoration is the drying and repair of cabinets, flooring, walls and subfloor after a kitchen leak. If water is spreading from a sink, dishwasher, refrigerator line or supply valve in your Verona home, shut off the water and call the 24/7 line. Kitchens pack plumbing, appliances and cabinetry into one space, so a leak does more damage than it looks like.

Appliance failures on their own are on the appliance leak page. This page is about what the water did to the kitchen.

Kitchen leak: what to do first

Close the shutoff valve under the sink or the one behind the appliance. If you cannot find it, close the main valve. Move small items off the floor and open the cabinet doors so air reaches the inside.

In the first minutes

  • Close the shutoff valve at the leak, or the main valve.
  • Unplug the dishwasher or refrigerator if the cord is dry and you can reach it.
  • Empty the cabinet under the sink and open the doors.
  • Mop standing water and move rugs.
  • Photograph the leak, cabinets and floor.

We answer nights and weekends. A crew can be sent the same day when one is available.

Why kitchen water damage spreads

Water under a sink base runs into the toe kick and under the flooring. Cabinet boxes made of particleboard swell when they get wet and often cannot be dried back to shape. Water in the subfloor can stay hidden below tile or vinyl.

Behind the cabinets, a wet wall cavity can feed mold if it is not opened and dried. Moisture meters and thermal imaging show where the water went.

Sink supply and drain

Loose or cracked lines under the sink leak slowly or fast.

Dishwasher

Hoses, door seals and pumps leak onto the floor.

Refrigerator ice maker line

A small line can leak for days behind the unit.

Overflow

A clogged sink or an unattended basin spills onto the counter and floor.

Kitchens in older and newer Verona homes

Older kitchens may have solid wood cabinets and older supply valves that stick or fail. Newer ones may use particleboard boxes and plastic supply lines. The material decides whether a wet cabinet can be dried or has to be replaced.

What affects kitchen water damage cost

  • The time between the first drip and the shutoff.
  • Cabinet and countertop type.
  • Flooring type, such as tile, vinyl or wood.
  • Whether the leak reached walls.
  • Drying days and repair scope.

Sudden kitchen leaks are often covered by homeowners insurance, and slow leaks from poor maintenance often are not. Check your policy and keep photos. Want your real number? Call (608) 871-6605.

Small steps that limit kitchen damage

Check under the sink once a month for dampness, and look behind the dishwasher and refrigerator when you clean. A bowl of water under a leaking trap, a stained cabinet floor or a soft toe kick are early signs. Replace braided supply lines that are old, and know where the shutoff valves are before you need them.

The EPA’s guidelines on moisture and mold advise fixing leaks quickly and drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours. That applies to cabinet bases and subfloors as much as to walls.

Can wet cabinets be saved?

Solid wood sometimes can. Particleboard that has swelled usually cannot be dried back to shape.

Do you fix the plumbing?

A plumber fixes the line or appliance. We dry the kitchen and repair the damage.

Can water hide under kitchen flooring?

Yes. Water can sit in the subfloor under tile or vinyl, which is why moisture readings are taken.
