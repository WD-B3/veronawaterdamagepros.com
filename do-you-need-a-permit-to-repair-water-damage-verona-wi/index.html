Do You Need a Permit to Repair Water Damage in Verona, WI?

Whether a water damage repair needs a permit depends on the work, not the water. Here is how to think about it in Verona, WI, and who to ask.

Written by the Verona Water Damage Pros team. Published November 9, 2026.
DISPATCH LINE
(608) 871-6605
Call Now

Drying a house and rebuilding it are different jobs, and permit rules treat them differently. Extracting water and running dehumidifiers is rarely a permitted activity. Replacing wiring, plumbing or structure often is. This guide explains the pattern. Rules change by municipality and by project, so the City of Verona building department is the source for your address.

Work that usually does not need a permit

  • Extracting water and drying the structure.
  • Removing wet carpet, pad and loose materials.
  • Replacing drywall with the same material in the same place, in many places.
  • Painting, trim and cosmetic repairs.

Even for these, local rules vary. Confirm before you assume.

Work that often does need a permit

Type of workWhy a permit is often requiredWho usually pulls it
Electrical repairs or rewiringSafety of wiring in wet areasLicensed electrician.
Plumbing repair or replacementWater supply and drain safetyLicensed plumber.
Structural repairs, such as framing or joistsLoad bearing safetyGeneral contractor.
Furnace or water heater replacementGas, venting and combustion safetyHVAC or plumbing contractor.
Large rebuilds after a major lossCode compliance for the finished workGeneral contractor.

The contractor doing the work usually pulls the permit. A homeowner doing work on their own house may be allowed to pull one, depending on the municipality and the type of work. Ask who is responsible before the job starts, and get the answer in writing.

Work done without a required permit can lead to problems when you sell, refinance or file an insurance claim.

Verona permit sources

For any permit question, contact the City of Verona building department. It can tell you which work needs a permit at your address and which inspections follow.

Wisconsin also licenses certain trades at the state level. The Wisconsin Department of Safety and Professional Services is the source for which licenses apply to electrical, plumbing and HVAC work.

After a major event

After a large local event, such as the June 17, 2014 tornado that struck near Country View Elementary or the August 20 and 21, 2018 floods in western Dane County, building departments get busy. Building departments can be busy, so ask early and plan for delays in the rebuild phase.

Emergency work to protect the building, such as water removal and securing openings, usually does not wait for a permit. Ask the city if you are unsure.

Permits and insurance

Insurers may pay for permit fees as part of a covered repair, and some require proof that work was permitted. Ask your adjuster what documentation they need. Keep the permit and inspection records with your claim file.

Questions to ask the city

  • Does my planned repair need a permit?
  • Who must apply: me or the contractor?
  • What inspections will follow?
  • How long does review usually take?
  • What happens if work started before the permit?

Ask a restoration company which parts of the job are mitigation and drying, which are rebuild and which are done by a licensed trade. See water extraction and wall and drywall repair for the types of work involved. Crews serve Madison and Cross Plains as well, each with its own building department.

Do it yourself work and permits

If you plan to do some of the repair yourself, ask the city what is allowed. Some municipalities let owners do certain work on their own home, while others restrict electrical and plumbing to licensed trades. Inspections still apply in many cases.

Even if a permit is not required, building codes still apply to the finished work. Keep receipts and photos of what you did.

A simple order of operations

Step 01

Stop and dry

Water is removed and the structure is dried first, which rarely needs a permit.

Step 02

Scope the rebuild

A written scope lists the work, the trades and the permits.

Step 03

Apply and schedule

The responsible party applies, and inspections are scheduled.

Step 04

Close out

Final inspections are signed off, and you keep copies of the records.

Keep every record together, including the application, the inspection sign-offs and any correction notices. A tidy file saves time with the insurer, a lender or a future buyer.

That order protects the house and your paperwork. It also keeps drying from being delayed by a permit that is not needed for mitigation.

If the city asks for drawings or a contractor license number, the contractor supplies them. Ask to see copies before the work begins.

The Wisconsin Department of Safety and Professional Services publishes licensing guidelines for the trades. The City of Verona building department is the source for permit rules at your address.

Permit questions from Verona homeowners

Who do I call in Verona to ask about a permit?

The City of Verona building department. Describe the work and ask what applies at your address.

Do I need a permit to replace drywall in Verona?

Often not for like for like drywall, but rules vary. Ask the city before you assume.

Is the permit my responsibility or the contractor’s?

Usually the contractor’s, but agree on it in writing before the job starts.

Will skipping a permit affect my insurance claim or a sale?

It can. Insurers and buyers sometimes ask for permit records.
