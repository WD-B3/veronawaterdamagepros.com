How to Read a Water Damage Scope of Work in Verona, WI

A scope of work lists what a crew will do and with what. Here is how to read one, line by line, if you are a Verona, WI homeowner.

Written by the Verona Water Damage Pros team. Published November 9, 2026.

A scope of work can run a page or ten. It is written in shorthand, with codes, abbreviations and quantities that are easy to skim past. Reading it carefully is the best protection you have against surprises. This guide walks through the parts in the order they usually appear.

Header and cause of loss

The top of the scope states the address, the date, the cause of loss and the water category. Check each. If the cause says burst pipe and it was actually a roof leak, the scope may be built on the wrong assumption. If the category says clean water and the source was a sewer line, that matters a lot.

Common terms and what they mean

TermPlain meaning
ExtractionRemoving standing water, with pumps or wet vacuums.
Flood cutCutting drywall a set height above the floor to reach a wet cavity.
Tear outRemoving wet materials that cannot be dried.
Air moverA fan that moves air across wet surfaces.
DehumidifierRemoves moisture from the air so materials can release water.
Moisture mappingRecording readings across surfaces to see where water is.
ContainmentPlastic barriers that keep dust or contaminants in one area.
Cat 1, Cat 2, Cat 3Water categories, from clean to heavily contaminated.

Reading the line items

Each line should name a room, a task, a quantity and a unit. A line such as living room, remove carpet pad, 250 square feet tells you what and where. A line such as demolition, lump sum tells you nothing.

Check that the rooms listed match the rooms where you saw water. If a room is missing, ask why.

Equipment lines

Equipment is often billed per piece per day. The scope should list the type, the number and the planned days. Ask how the number of units was chosen. In a well run job, equipment count follows the size of the wet area and the volume of the room, not a standard number.

Ask when equipment will be checked and who will remove it. Equipment should come out when readings show dry, not on a fixed date.

Monitoring and the dry standard

  • How often readings are taken, such as daily.
  • What reading counts as dry for each material.
  • Where the readings will be recorded and how you can see them.
  • What happens if a reading stalls.

This section is the most important and the most often missing. Without it, you cannot tell when drying is done.

Verona notes for your scope

Normals for Madison from 1991 to 2020 give a January mean of 19.4°F and 51.8 inches of snow a year. In cold months, a closed house dries differently from a humid summer one, so ask how the crew adjusts drying for the season.

Ask the City of Verona building department about any permit for rebuild work in the scope, and ask the contractor who pulls it.

Exclusions and allowances

Look for words like not included, by others or allowance. These show what the scope does not cover. A rebuild line marked by others means another contractor does that work. An allowance is a stand-in amount that can change.

If the scope excludes something you assumed was included, ask about it before work starts.

Change orders

Keep your signed scope and every change order in one place. If the final invoice differs from the plan, the paper trail shows when and why each change was approved.

A change order is a written change to the scope, usually because something new was found. It should state what changed, why and what it costs. Do not agree to extra work by phone alone. Ask for the change in writing, even if it is a short text.

For examples of what drying work includes, see structural drying and water extraction. Crews serve Oregon and Mount Horeb as well.

A quick checklist

  • Does the cause and category match what you saw?
  • Is every wet room listed?
  • Does each line show a task, a quantity and a unit?
  • Are equipment types, counts and days stated?
  • Is there a monitoring plan and a dry standard?
  • Are exclusions and change orders explained?

Photos and logs that belong with the scope

A scope is more convincing with photos attached. Ask for before photos of each room, photos of the equipment placed and a log of daily readings. These show that the work followed the plan and give your adjuster the evidence behind each line. Keep copies in the same folder as the scope.

Scope of work questions from Verona homeowners

What does flood cut mean in a scope?

It means cutting drywall a few inches or feet above the floor to dry a wet cavity. The height depends on where the wicking stops.

Can I ask to see the moisture readings?

Yes. A company that logs readings can share them. Ask how often they are taken.

What if the scope has no dry standard?

Ask the company to add one. Without it, there is no clear point at which drying is finished.

Who in Verona can review a scope with me?

Your insurance adjuster, a second restoration company or a general contractor can read it with you. Your agent can explain how it fits your policy.
