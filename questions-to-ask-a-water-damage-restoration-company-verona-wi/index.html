Questions to Ask a Water Damage Restoration Company in Verona, WI

Questions to ask a water damage restoration company before you hire, with what a good answer sounds like. Written for Verona, WI homeowners.

Written by the Verona Water Damage Pros team. Published November 9, 2026.

Choosing a restoration company while water is spreading is stressful, and that is when people accept the first answer they hear. A few good questions help you tell a careful company from a rushed one. Ask any company these, including us.

Questions about scope and price

  • What is included in your scope, room by room?
  • How is the price set: by equipment, by day, by area or by item?
  • What could change the price after work starts, and how will I be told?
  • Will I get the scope in writing before work begins?

A good answer is specific. Vague answers about the job being hard to price are a warning, since a crew can measure and meter before it quotes. No honest company should promise a final figure before it has seen the water.

Questions about the work

  • What moisture readings will you take, and how often?
  • What equipment will you use, and how many pieces?
  • How will I know the structure is dry before anything is closed up?
  • Will you remove materials, and which ones? Why?
  • What happens if the readings do not drop?

Drying is measured, not guessed. A company that cannot tell you its readings or its target for dry is not tracking. See structural drying for what that tracking looks like.

  • Will you document the job with photos and a log I can give to my adjuster?
  • Do you work directly with insurers, and will you explain the scope to my adjuster?
  • What do you need from me to start?
  • Do you have general liability coverage, and can you show proof on request?

Coverage is your insurer’s decision, not the restoration company’s. Be cautious of anyone who promises that your claim will be approved.

Questions about standards

The IICRC publishes standards for water damage restoration, known as S500, and for mold, known as S520. It is fair to ask any company whether its crews are trained to those standards and how that shows up in the job. We treat these as standards to ask about, not a credential to take on faith.

Local checks for Verona homeowners

Ask the City of Verona building department whether your repair work needs a permit, and ask your insurer what it requires from a restoration company.

After a large event such as the August 20 and 21, 2018 flooding in western Dane County, companies get busy and some arrive from out of the area. Ask where the crew is based and who will be on site.

Questions about timing

Ask when a crew can start, but expect an honest answer, not a promise. Same-day response is often possible, and it depends on the day and the crews. A company that gives you a firm time before it knows the situation is guessing.

Ask who answers the phone at night and whether the dispatch line is staffed 24 hours a day.

Our dispatch line is answered at all hours and a crew can be sent the same day when one is available. For a flooded lower level, see basement water damage cleanup. Crews also serve Madison and Cross Plains.

What a good first call sounds like

A useful first call is calm and specific. The dispatcher asks where the water started, how much there is, whether power is involved and whether anyone can reach the main valve. You get instructions you can follow right away, such as which valve to close and what to move.

You should also hear what happens next: when a crew can be sent, what the crew will check first and how the scope and price will be explained before work starts.

If a company only wants your address and credit card, hang up and call another. Price talk comes after the crew sees the water, not before.

Write down the name of the person you spoke with and the time of the call. If the crew arrives and the story has changed, you have a record to point to.

Red flags during the first visit

  • Pressure to sign before the crew has seen the water.
  • No meters or cameras, only a quick look.
  • A refusal to put the scope in writing.
  • Promises about what your insurer will pay.

Hiring questions from Verona homeowners

Should I get more than one estimate?

If time allows, yes. With active water, do not wait long, because the damage grows. Ask each company for the scope in writing.

Do I have to use the company my insurer suggests?

Generally you can choose your own contractor. Ask your insurer about its process.

How do I check whether a company is local?

Ask where the crew is based and whether the dispatch line is staffed locally. Ask for a business address.

Is a company with a lower price better?

Not necessarily. Compare scopes, not totals. A low price with a thin scope can cost more after drying fails.
