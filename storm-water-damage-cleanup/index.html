Storm water damage cleanup is the removal of water and drying of the structure after rain, wind or hail lets water into a home. If a storm has opened your roof, flooded your basement or pushed water through a window in Verona, call the 24/7 line. Storms hit many homes at once, so call early.

This page is about water that arrives with a storm. Roof leaks that start inside are on the ceiling page.

After the storm: what to do first

Make sure everyone is safe, and stay away from downed lines and flooded electrical panels. Stop more water from coming in if you can safely do it, such as moving items away from a leak or covering an opening. Photograph the damage before you clean up.

After a storm

  • Stay clear of downed lines and standing water near outlets.
  • Photograph the damage inside and out.
  • Move belongings away from leaks and standing water.
  • Cover openings only if you can do it safely from the ground or inside.
  • Call your insurer and the 24/7 line.

After a big storm, call early

Large storms create many calls at once. Describe what you can see, how much water there is and whether anyone has been hurt. If an opening is large, a roofer or a board-up service may be needed in addition to drying. We handle the water and the structure, and other trades handle the shell.

Keep receipts for anything you buy to protect the property, such as tarps and plywood. Many policies reimburse reasonable emergency costs.

Documenting storm damage

Photograph the exterior first, then each room. Take wide shots and close ups of ceiling stains, wet walls and standing water. Write down the date and time the storm hit. A simple log of what you saw and when helps your adjuster and any contractor who arrives later.

Storm damage questions

Do you handle storm damage at night or on weekends in Verona?

Yes. Call any hour of the day. A crew is sent the same day when one can be spared. After large storms, call early.

Do you repair the roof?

No. Roofers fix the roof. We remove the water and dry the structure.

Does homeowners insurance cover flood damage?

Usually not. Flood needs its own policy. Look up the FEMA flood map and talk to your agent.

Should I wait for the adjuster before cleanup?

Photograph everything first. Water that sits adds damage, so ask your insurer how to proceed.
