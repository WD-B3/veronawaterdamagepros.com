Why hills change the water problem

In hill country, rain does not spread out. It runs downhill and collects. A house below a slope or at the base of a driveway takes more runoff than one on level ground. Grading, downspouts and window wells decide whether that water goes around the house or against it.

Snowmelt does the same thing in spring. If your basement shows dampness or standing water after a thaw, that is a runoff pattern, not a one-time event.

Winter and the spring melt in the hills

Snow on a slope melts and runs toward the lowest ground. A basement wall that stays dry in July can seep in March when the soil is saturated and the ground below is still frozen. Water that cannot soak in goes sideways, and sideways means against your foundation.

Frozen downspout extensions make it worse. If the discharge point is iced over, roof water drops at the wall instead of draining away.
