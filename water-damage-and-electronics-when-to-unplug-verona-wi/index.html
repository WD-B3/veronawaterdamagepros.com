Water Damage and Electronics: When to Unplug in Verona, WI

Water and power is the dangerous mix. This guide explains when to unplug, when not to touch, and what to do with wet electronics in Verona, WI homes.

Written by the Verona Water Damage Pros team. Published November 9, 2026.

Water and electricity do not mix, and the first rule is about you, not the devices. Do not touch a plug, power strip or panel if you are standing in water or if the area is wet. Once you are safe, the second question is what to do with the electronics themselves.

Safety first: before anything is unplugged

  • Stay out of standing water if outlets, cords or power strips are submerged.
  • If the main panel is wet or in the water, do not touch it. Call the utility or 911.
  • Turn off power at the breaker only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water.
  • Unplug devices only when you are on dry ground and the plug and cord are dry.
  • If you smell burning or see sparks, leave and call 911.

When in doubt, wait for a qualified electrician. No television is worth an injury.

What happens to wet electronics

Water on circuit boards can short them out or corrode contacts over hours and days. Clean water is easier to deal with than flood water, which carries minerals and contaminants. Salt and sewage are the worst for corrosion.

A device that works after it dried may fail later. The damage can be slow.

Do not use the rice trick

Putting a wet phone in rice is a common tip that does not hold up well. Rice does not pull water out of the inside. A technician can open the device and dry it properly. If the device holds data, back it up as soon as it is safe to turn on, or ask a technician to recover it.

Verona electrical notes

The National Weather Service recorded an EF-3 tornado near Country View Elementary in Verona on June 17, 2014. Storm outages and power surges often come with wet weather, so surge protection and unplugging before storms are worth considering.

Ask the City of Verona building department whether electrical repairs in your home need a permit, and use a licensed electrician for panel work.

Dry the room to protect what is nearby

Wet rooms stay humid for days and keep slowly damaging electronics nearby. Remove standing water with water extraction, then dry the room with dehumidifiers and air movers. See structural drying for what that process looks like.

Move electronics to a dry, ventilated room while the area dries. Keep them off the floor and away from damp walls.

Leave a note on any device that was wet, such as a sticky label saying do not power on. It stops a family member from plugging it in out of habit.

Report and document

Photograph each device, its label and the damage before it is removed. List the make and model. Your insurer may cover electronics under personal property coverage, which often has its own deductible and limits.

Ask your agent whether surge damage and water damage are treated separately.

Surges, storms and prevention

Water is not the only way electronics are lost in a storm. Surges from nearby strikes or grid problems can burn out a power supply. Surge protectors help but do wear out, and a protector that has absorbed a big hit may no longer be protecting anything. Replace them after a major event, and unplug sensitive devices during a severe storm if you are home.

Keep electronics off basement floors. A shelf above the floor protects against a few inches of water.

Back up important files regularly to a drive that is not in the same room as the computer, and to a cloud service. A backup is the cheapest insurance for the one thing money cannot replace.

Manufacturer guidelines and standard electrical safety advice are to keep wet electronics off until a technician has checked them.

Electronics questions from Verona homeowners

Is it safe to plug in a TV that got wet in Verona?

Not until it has dried and been checked by a technician. Plugging in a damp device can cause a short or a fire.

Should I turn off the breaker for a flooded basement?

Only if the panel is dry and you can reach it from dry ground. Otherwise call the utility or 911 and do not enter the water.

Can I save the data on a wet computer?

Often, if you do not power it on. A data recovery technician can open it and extract the drive.

Does insurance cover wet electronics?

Often under personal property coverage, with limits and deductibles. Ask your agent.
